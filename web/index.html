Take Hachi with you

The Olfa browser extension checks every page you open and every link you hover, and stops you before you hand a password to a fake site.

⬇️ Download extension

Install in 4 steps

  1. Download the zip above and unzip it. You get a folder called olfa-extension. Keep it somewhere permanent, because Chrome reads it from there.
  2. In Chrome, Edge or Brave open the address chrome://extensions and switch on Developer mode (top right).
  3. Click Load unpacked and choose the unzipped olfa-extension folder.
  4. Pin Olfa from the puzzle-piece menu. Open any website: Hachi appears in the corner and the toolbar icon shows the safety score.

Page checks

Fake login forms, hidden redirects and look-alike domains are caught as the page loads.

🚧

Block page

The four checks

🧠

Link analysis

A machine-learning model reads the link itself: its length, odd characters, how it is built, and whether it imitates a well-known brand. Established popular domains are treated with care to avoid false alarms.

🌐

Web page check

Olfa follows redirects and looks at the page for password forms that send data elsewhere, hidden frames and other tricks used by phishing kits.

🔒

Certificate and age

It checks the site's security certificate and how recently the domain was registered. Brand-new domains asking for logins are a common warning sign.

👥

Community intelligence

Links reported by other people are remembered. The Community tab shows recent reports, and your reports help the next person.

What the verdicts mean

✅ SafeNo warning signs found. Hachi wags her tail.
⚠️ SuspiciousSome signs look off. Be careful with logins, payments and downloads.
⛔ DangerousStrong evidence of phishing or malware. The extension shows a block page.

Good to know

Works even when the service is off

The extension has built-in offline rules for look-alike domains and page tricks. The toolbar shows "Offline checks only" when it cannot reach the Olfa service.

Explained, not just scored

Every verdict lists the reasons behind it, so you can judge for yourself instead of trusting a number.

No tool is perfect

Olfa lowers your risk but cannot promise to catch every scam. A brand-new attack may score as safe, so if something feels wrong, trust that feeling and report it.
